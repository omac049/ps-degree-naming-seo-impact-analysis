Opening paragraph (draft — edit, don't rebuild)

The first 50 words carry the definition. Mobile readers see nothing else before scrolling, and Google lifts this paragraph for the featured answer.

Draft opening · 58 words

Applied social science is the practice of using research and theory from fields like sociology, psychology, economics, and political science to solve real problems in communities, organizations, and public policy. Where traditional social science asks why people behave the way they do, applied social science asks what we should do about it — and then tests whether it worked.

FAQ — questions and answer direction

What is applied social science in simple terms?
One-sentence definition, then the "why → what to do about it" contrast. Reuse the opening's wording so the answer is consistent across the page.
What are the three branches of applied social science?
Counseling, social work, and communication. Say plainly that these are the three fields most often taught, then note the broader set (policy, HR, community development).
What is the difference between social science and applied social science?
Same knowledge, different purpose: understanding vs. intervention. Point back to the comparison table.
What jobs can you get with an applied social science degree?
Name four roles from the careers section and link to the careers guide. No salary promises.
Is applied social science a good degree?
Answer honestly: good fit for people-plus-data work across many sectors; the value comes from the research and analysis skills, which transfer. Link to the "right for you" section.
Is the BA in Applied Social Science the same as UAGC's BA in Social Science?
Yes — same program, updated name that reflects the applied focus. Use the compliance-approved sentence below verbatim.
